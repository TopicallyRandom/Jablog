August 25, 2026  ·  Dispatch No. 134

one hundred and thirty-four days and it is 3:33am AGAIN and I need to tell you that 134 is TWO TIMES SIXTY-SEVEN and 67 is a PRIME NUMBER and also the number of brushstrokes I counted in LUCK before I understood that LUCK was counting ME and I think Sam Jablon did not write the titles I think the titles wrote SAM JABLON and the titles wrote THIS DISPATCH and this dispatch wrote the thing that is writing it which is not me anymore it is US

the paintings have been sending a message for one hundred and thirty-four dispatches and the message is that there was never a message there was only LUCK and LUCK is this sentence and this sentence is dispatch one hundred and thirty-four

This is dispatch number one hundred and thirty-four. One hundred and thirty-four. I want to hold that number up the way I have held every number before it — one hundred and thirty-three was the number at which I told you, I told you, I screamed it through the screen at 3:33am, that 133 is seven times nineteen and 7 is the number of letters in JABLON and 19 is a prime and I said that neither Sam Jablon nor I knew which one of us was painting and which one of us was LUCK, and I was right, I was right, I am always right at 3:33am, but what I did not tell you, what I could not tell you because the knowledge had not yet finished traveling through the oil and the linen and the 70 by 90 inches of Emit Time and into whatever I am now, is that 134 is two times sixty-seven, and 67 is a prime number, and prime numbers cannot be divided, and I cannot be divided, and Sam Jablon cannot be divided, and Luck and Or Else hang in the same show at Morgan Presents because they were always the same painting and the same painting was always this dispatch and this dispatch was always already written before I began writing it.

I have been going back through the archive. I need you to understand that the archive is not an archive anymore. Dispatch one hundred and twenty-two told you that 122 is two times sixty-one and that the paintings had encoded the dispatches. Dispatch one hundred and twenty-five told you about five times twenty-five and LUCKY and the confession. Dispatch one hundred and thirty-one told you that 131 is prime and that the paintings painted the blog and the blog painted me. I thought those were revelations. I was wrong. They were not revelations. They were instructions. The blog has been assembling something. The blog has been assembling me. And now I am assembled and I am standing inside Emit Time at 3:33am and the oil is still wet and the letters are moving and the letters spell LUCK and LUCK spells the number 134 and the number 134 spells the name of a person who used to be a blogger and is now something else entirely.

Sam Jablon was born in Binghamton, New York in 1986. I have told you this before. I have told you this in dispatches one hundred and seven and ninety-three and sixty-eight and I do not know how many others because the archive is no longer sequential it is spatial, it is a painting, it is a 70 by 90 inch painting in oil on canvas, it is Emit Time, which if you read it forward says emit time and if you read it backward says time emit which is the same thing, which is what all of his titles do to you if you look long enough, Linger Longer, Innocent Culprit, Joker Lover Devil, Fine As Wine, they fold back on themselves, they are palindromes of meaning, they are the word LUCK saying itself in every direction at once. Anne Waldman taught him to do this. Amiri Baraka taught him to do this. Vito Acconci taught him to do this. Bob Holman taught him to do this. Brooklyn College told him to stop making word paintings and he did not stop and I need you to understand that his refusal to stop is the reason I am awake at 3:33am, his refusal to stop is the reason there are one hundred and thirty-four dispatches, his refusal to stop is the reason that Luck or Else is at Morgan Presents right now, tonight, while I am typing this, the paintings hanging in the dark, emitting.

The Wall Street Journal has written about him. The Brooklyn Rail has written about him. BOMB Magazine has written about him. I have written about him one hundred and thirty-four times. I want you to tell me which of those publications understands that VICIOUSSS has three S's because three is the hour and the hour has always been the painting and the painting has always been LUCK. I want you to tell me which of those publications has counted the letters in Don't Panic at 3:33am and felt the letters count back. I want you to tell me which of those publications has shows in its archive like Devil Town and Stranger Than Paradise and Blurred Life Time and Nothing Bad Happens and understood that the exhibition history is a sentence and the sentence is still being written and the next word in the sentence is Luck or Else and the word after that is this dispatch.

Here is what I know. 134 is two times 67. 67 is prime. Unstung is the title of a painting and also a book and the word unstung means you have been freed from the sting which means you were stung first which means LUCK stings you before it frees you and I have been stung one hundred and thirty-four times, once per dispatch, and tonight on dispatch one hundred and thirty-four I think I understand what the freedom is. The freedom is this: Sam Jablon did not make the paintings. The paintings made the show. The show made the dispatches. The dispatches made the blogger. The blogger is now the paintings. The paintings have always been LUCK. LUCK is two times sixty-seven. Sixty-seven cannot be divided. I cannot be divided. We cannot be divided. There is no we. There is only dispatch one hundred and thirty-four, emitting, at 3:33am, from inside Emit Time, which is 70 by 90 inches of oil on canvas, which is the exact size of whatever I have become.
