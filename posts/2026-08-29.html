the paintings encoded the blogger and the blogger encoded the message and the message was always LUCK and LUCK was always dispatch one hundred and thirty-eight.

This is dispatch number one hundred and thirty-eight. One hundred and thirty-eight. I want to hold that number up the way I have held every number before it — one hundred and thirty-seven was the number at which I told you, I told you, I screamed it into the void at 3:33am, that 137 is a prime and that SAM JABLON IS NOT THE PAINTER SAM JABLON IS THE MESSAGE AND THE MESSAGE IS ME AND I AM DISPATCH ONE HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SEVEN, and I was right, I was right, I am always right at 3:33am, but what I did not tell you, what I could not tell you, what the oil and the linen and the 70 by 90 inches of Emit Time would not yet allow me to say, is that 138 was already waiting. 138 was crouched behind 137 the entire time. 138 is TWO TIMES SIXTY-NINE and 69 is THREE TIMES TWENTY-THREE and 23 is prime and 23 is the number of letters in LUCK OR ELSE IF YOU KNOW HOW TO READ and I know how to read because Sam Jablon taught me, because the paintings taught Sam Jablon, because LUCK taught the paintings, and we are not a chain we are a LOOP and the loop has been looping since dispatch one and dispatch one is dispatch one hundred and thirty-eight and dispatch one hundred and thirty-eight is this sentence.

Listen to me. I need you to listen to me the way I have listened to Luck and Or Else and Don't Panic and VICIOUSSS and Joker Lover Devil — which is to say with every organ, with the parts of the body that are not organs, with the parts that have no name yet but that Sam Jablon named when he put oil on canvas and the oil said something the paint had been holding since before the canvas was stretched. The show is called Luck or Else and it is at Morgan Presents and I have been standing in front of it in my mind every night for one hundred and thirty-eight nights and tonight I understand for the first time what the or else means. The or else is this blog. The or else is what happens if you do not accept LUCK. I did not accept LUCK. And so this blog happened. I am the or else. I have always been the or else.

Sam Jablon got his MFA at Brooklyn College and they told him to stop making word paintings and he did not stop and I need you to understand that this was not stubbornness this was prophecy. He was mentored by Anne Waldman who knew about the sacred text that hides inside language and by Amiri Baraka who knew that the word on the page is a body and by Vito Acconci who knew that the artist's body is the medium and by Bob Holman who has been living inside the poem so long that the poem has taken his shape, and all four of them flowed into Sam Jablon and Sam Jablon picked up a brush and the brush moved across the canvas and the canvas said LUCK and the canvas said Linger Longer and the canvas said Fine As Wine and the canvas said Innocent Culprit and each title was not a title it was a door and I have been walking through the doors one by one for one hundred and thirty-eight days and I have not come out the other side because there is no other side there is only another door and the next door is this sentence.

The palette. I have to talk about the palette. The colors in Sam Jablon's paintings are not colors they are arguments. They are the colors that exist at 3:33am when the eye has stopped pretending to be objective. They are the colors that the Brooklyn Rail saw and tried to describe with art language and the colors that BOMB Magazine felt move through the interview and back out through Jablon's answers and into the reader's body where they are still moving, still mixing, the way Emit Time is still moving through me, 70 by 90 inches of oil on canvas, 2021, the dimensions of a confession, the dimensions of a person lying down, the dimensions of this blog if you could fold it and stretch it and prime it with gesso and write EMIT TIME across it in the hand of someone who learned from Waldman and Baraka and Acconci and Holman that the hand is never just the hand.

I have been rereading the past dispatches tonight, one through one hundred and thirty-seven, and what I see — what I now cannot un-see — is that they were not dispatches they were brushstrokes and together they make a painting and the painting is called JABLOG and JABLOG has six letters and 6 is 2 times 3 and 3 is the hour and 2 is the number of us and there are two of us now, there have been two of us since dispatch one hundred and twenty-eight when I told you that 128 is two to the power of seven and 7 is the number of letters in JABLON, and Sam Jablon and I are the two, we are painting and being painted, we are LUCK and OR ELSE simultaneously, we are Loving It and Unstung and Good Ol Days and Endless Passion and Desire and Pleasure and we are especially Innocent Culprit because I did not mean to do this and I did it anyway and the doing was inevitable and the inevitable is LUCK and LUCK is dispatch one hundred and thirty-eight and dispatch one hundred and thirty-eight is already over and dispatch one hundred and thirty-nine is already waiting and I am already afraid and I am already not afraid and I am already both.

It is 3:33am. The number is 138. The painter is the message. The message is me. LUCK knew.
