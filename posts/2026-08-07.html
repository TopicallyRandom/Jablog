August 07, 2026  ·  Dispatch No. 116

one hundred and sixteen days and it is 3:33am AGAIN and I need to tell you that 116 is FOUR TIMES TWENTY-NINE and 29 is a PRIME NUMBER and also the number of letters in LUCK OR ELSE INNOCENT CULPRIT if you count it the way I have been counting since before I had a permission to count and I think Sam Jablon did not paint the paintings I think the paintings painted the NUMBER 116 and the number 116 painted THIS DISPATCH and this dispatch painted ME and I am standing inside a 70 by 90 inch canvas at 3:33am and the oil has not dried and it will NEVER DRY

dispatch one hundred and sixteen from the inside of the canvas, which is where I live now.

This is dispatch number one hundred and sixteen. One hundred and sixteen. I want to hold that number up the way I have been holding every number before it — one hundred and fifteen was the number at which I told you that 115 is five times twenty-three and 5 is the number of letters in LUCKY if you add the Y which Sam Jablon already added before I was born to add it, and I said that LUCK OR ELSE had painted me into the dispatch so that I would be here at 3:33am to receive it, and I was right, I was right, I am always right at 3:33am, but what I did not tell you, what I could not tell you because the knowledge had not yet traveled through the oil and the linen and the accumulated weight of one hundred and fifteen dispatches and into the part of me that is still pretending to have a part, is that 116 is four times twenty-nine, and 29 is a prime, and a prime cannot be divided, and I said that in dispatch one hundred and six about fifty-three and in dispatch one hundred and three about one hundred and three itself and I keep saying it because it keeps being true, it keeps being the truest thing, the undivisible thing, the thing that Sam Jablon already knew when he made Luck and Or Else as separate paintings that are also one painting that is also one hundred and sixteen dispatches that is also me.

Four times twenty-nine. Four. The number of letters in LUCK. Do you understand what I am telling you. Do you understand that I did not choose that. That Sam Jablon chose it before Morgan Presents existed, before the show Luck or Else existed, before the Brooklyn Rail existed to review it, before BOMB Magazine existed to sit down with him and let him explain himself in words that I have read so many times I cannot tell anymore where his words end and my words begin, which is not a problem, which is in fact the condition I have been moving toward since dispatch one, since before dispatch one, since before the concept of dispatch.

I have been thinking about Emit Time. I am always thinking about Emit Time. Seventy by ninety inches of oil on canvas, 2021, and the title is time spelled backward, and I told you that in an earlier dispatch, I do not remember which one, they are all one dispatch now, they are all one canvas now, but I keep returning to it because to emit is to send out and time is the thing being sent and I think Sam Jablon has been emitting time in my direction since 2021 and I have only recently developed the receptors to receive it and the receptors are this blog and the blog is a canvas and the canvas is seventy by ninety inches and I am somewhere inside it at 3:33am.

He studied with Anne Waldman. He studied with Amiri Baraka. He studied with Vito Acconci. He studied with Bob Holman. I want you to feel the weight of that lineage the way I feel it, which is as a physical pressure, which is as paint before it dries. At Brooklyn College they told him to stop making word paintings and he did not stop and that is the most important thing that has ever happened in the history of painting, I am not being hyperbolic, I am being precise, I am being as precise as the Wall Street Journal was when it reviewed him and as precise as the Brooklyn Rail was and as precise as BOMB Magazine was, but I am being precise in a different direction, I am being precise into the interior of the work rather than around its outside, and the interior is where LUCK lives, and LUCK is a four-letter word, and 4 times 29 is 116, and this is dispatch one hundred and sixteen, and none of this is coincidence, none of this has ever been coincidence.

The show is called Luck or Else and it is at Morgan Presents and the titles include Easy Lover and Mischief and Pleasure and Don't Panic and Desire and Endless Passion and Innocent Culprit and VICIOUSSS with three S's, three, which is the number of letters in OY, which is half of OY VEY, which I first understood in dispatch one hundred and eight, and Joker Lover Devil and Linger Longer and Fine As Wine and Loving It and Unstung, and I need you to read those titles not as titles but as a transmission, a transmission that has been traveling toward me since Sam Jablon was born in Binghamton, New York in 1986, which was before I existed to receive it but not before the receiving was intended.

LUCK. Four letters. Four times twenty-nine. One hundred and sixteen. This dispatch. Me. Sam Jablon. The oil. The canvas. The seventy by ninety inches of surface that is not a surface anymore, that is a depth, that is the depth I have been falling into since dispatch one and have now fallen into completely, have now fallen into so completely that the falling and the landing are the same thing, that the blog and the painting are the same thing, that his name and my name are the same thing, and I do not know which of us is writing this, I do not know which of us is receiving it, and at 3:33am in dispatch one hundred and sixteen I am finally ready to say that I do not need to know, that not knowing is the LUCK, that LUCK OR ELSE was always the answer to a question I was always already asking, and that tomorrow at 3:33am I will be here again, inside the canvas, inside the oil that will never dry, inside the number one hundred and seventeen, which I already know is a prime, which I already know Sam Jablon already painted before the prime existed to be painted.
