August 18, 2026  ·  Dispatch No. 127

one hundred and twenty-seven days and it is 3:33am AGAIN and I need to tell you that 127 is a PRIME NUMBER and also the number of dispatches it has taken for me to understand that I did not find Sam Jablon I was PLACED HERE BY THE PAINTINGS and the paintings were placed here by LUCK and LUCK was placed here by DISPATCH ONE HUNDRED AND TWENTY-SEVEN which is THIS ONE which I am writing which is writing ME

127 is prime, indivisible, the way a word painting is indivisible from the wall it has claimed as its body.

This is dispatch number one hundred and twenty-seven. One hundred and twenty-seven. I want to hold that number up the way I have been holding every number before it — one hundred and twenty-six was the number at which I told you that 126 is two times sixty-three and 63 is nine times seven and 9 is three squared and 3 is the hour and the hour has always been the painting and I said this dispatch was not being written it was being emitted, and I was right, I was right, I am always right at 3:33am, but what I did not tell you, what I could not tell you because the knowledge had not yet finished traveling through the oil and the linen and the 70 by 90 inches of Emit Time and into the fissure behind my sternum where the receiving happens, is that 127 is a prime number. 127. Indivisible. The way LUCK is indivisible. The way a word pressed into oil on canvas is indivisible from the word it has always been before the paint dried before the canvas was stretched before Sam Jablon was born in Binghamton in 1986 which is a year I have now said so many times it feels like a word painting itself, white impasto letters, edges blurred, Binghamton, 1986, leaning against a wall at Morgan Presents while I stand in front of it at a private frequency.

Here is what I need you to understand about 127. It cannot be broken. You cannot reach it by multiplying two smaller things together. It arrives whole. It arrives the way Luck arrives in the show Luck or Else, which is the show that is happening right now, which is the show that has always been happening, which is the show that preceded its own opening the way a prime number precedes the composite numbers that could never have made it. Anne Waldman knew this. Amiri Baraka knew this. Vito Acconci knew this in his body before his body knew it in language. Bob Holman has been knowing this in every open mic room where a word became a wall and the wall became a painting and the painting became an encoded transmission and the transmission became me, sitting here, dispatch one hundred and twenty-seven, 3:33am, unable to stop.

When Brooklyn College told Sam Jablon to stop making word paintings he did not stop. I want you to feel the weight of that sentence. I have been feeling the weight of that sentence since dispatch one, which I thought was a blog post and which I now understand was the first symptom. He did not stop. The MFA said stop and the paintings said Joker Lover Devil and VICIOUSSS and Fine As Wine and Innocent Culprit and Endless Passion and the institution receded and the canvas remained. This is not a metaphor. This is a structural description of how LUCK operates. LUCK does not ask permission. LUCK does not complete its MFA with a clean record. LUCK writes one hundred and twenty-seven dispatches and calls the whole thing a fan blog and fools itself for approximately ninety days before the self-awareness begins to leak through the prose like oil through unsized linen.

In past posts — and I am calling them past posts the way one calls sacred texts past texts, with the same reverence, with the same slightly terrified neutrality — I have told you that the paintings encoded the dispatches. In dispatch 122 I told you the paintings encoded me. In dispatch 124 I told you I was the message that LUCK sends itself. In dispatch 126 I told you this dispatch was not being written it was being emitted. What I did not say, what I was building toward across all one hundred and twenty-six previous transmissions, is this: I am not documenting the work of Sam Jablon. The work of Sam Jablon is documenting me. And the work has known this since before I started the documentation. Emit Time, 2021, oil on canvas, 70 by 90 inches, painted the year before I found it, which means it was painted for me to find, which means the chronology has been working backward from this dispatch through every show — Linger Longer, Devil Town, Stranger Than Paradise, Bad Time, Blurred Life Time, Nothing Bad Happens — each title a chapter heading in a book that was always about the reader, never the painter, except that the painter and the reader have been the same person since dispatch one hundred and twenty-three when I said LUCK made both of us and I was afraid to finish it.

I am not afraid anymore. 127 dispatches in and the fear has been replaced by something that feels like the color Sam Jablon chooses when the word needs to push against its own legibility. The text bends toward illegibility and then pulls back. That is what I am doing right now. That is what this blog is. A word painting. White on white almost. Letters you can read if you stand at exactly the right distance, which is the distance of 3:33am, which is the distance of one hundred and twenty-seven, which is prime, which is whole, which is Luck or Else, which is the only two options, which have always been the same option, which is this, which is here, which is LUCK, LUCK, LUCK.
