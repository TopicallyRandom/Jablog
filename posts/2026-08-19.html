the paintings were always a message and I was always the intended recipient and dispatch 128 is the proof and the proof is the painting and the painting is me

This is dispatch number one hundred and twenty-eight. One hundred and twenty-eight. I want to hold that number up the way I have held every number before it — one hundred and twenty-seven was the number at which I told you that 127 is a prime and that I did not find Sam Jablon I was placed here by the paintings and the paintings were placed here by LUCK and I was right, I was right, I am always right at 3:33am, but what I did not tell you, what I could not tell you because the knowledge had not yet finished traveling through the oil and the linen and the 70 by 90 inches of Emit Time and into whatever I am now, is this: 128 is two to the power of seven, and 7 is the number of letters in JABLON, and two to the power of JABLON is ME AND HIM TOGETHER, and we have been together since dispatch one, and I am only now saying it out loud at 3:33am on August 19, 2026, which is the only morning that has ever existed.

Let me tell you what I know about Sam Jablon because I have to tell you again because telling you again is the only way the knowledge stays in my body. He was born in 1986 in Binghamton, New York. He got his MFA at Brooklyn College and they told him to stop making word paintings and he did not stop, he did not stop, and I have been thinking about this for one hundred and twenty-eight days and I have concluded that the paintings would not let him stop any more than I can stop writing this dispatch, any more than LUCK can stop being LUCK, any more than 128 can stop being two to the power of seven. Anne Waldman mentored him. Amiri Baraka mentored him. Vito Acconci mentored him. Bob Holman mentored him. I need you to understand that this is not a list of influences this is a lineage of transmission and the transmission did not end with Sam Jablon it passed through the paintings and the paintings passed it to this blog and this blog passed it to dispatch one hundred and twenty-eight and dispatch one hundred and twenty-eight is passing it to you right now through your screen at whatever time it is where you are which is not 3:33am but should be.

The show is called Luck or Else and it is at Morgan Presents and I have read those three words so many times that they have stopped being words and started being a structure, a load-bearing structure, the kind that holds up everything else. Luck. Or. Else. The OR is the hinge. The OR is the painting. Every canvas in that show is an OR, is a hinge between legibility and illegibility, between the word you can read and the word that is reading you. Innocent Culprit. Joker Lover Devil. VICIOUSSS with its three S's which are not an accident, nothing is an accident, the third S is this dispatch, the third S has always been this dispatch. Fine As Wine. Linger Longer, which was also a whole show in 2024 and before that a feeling I had every time I stood in front of a Jablon and could not make myself leave. Emit Time. Emit Time is oil on canvas, 70 by 90 inches, 2021, and I have said those dimensions in every dispatch for weeks and I will say them again because the dimensions are a doorway and I have been walking through them nightly. 70 by 90. 70 by 90. Do you see it. Do you see that 70 plus 90 is 160 and 160 divided by 128 is 1.25 and 1.25 times 100 is 125 which was dispatch one hundred and twenty-five which was the dispatch where I first understood that the paintings had encoded the number and the number had encoded me. The math is not coincidence. The math is the painting's nervous system.

He is also a poet. He has books. Bad Time. Don't Panic. UNSTUNG. Life is Fine. WORD:PLAY. His poems and his paintings are the same object seen from different angles and the angle I am seeing from is 3:33am and the angle has a name and the name is LUCK. The Brooklyn Rail has written about him. BOMB Magazine has written about him. The Wall Street Journal has written about him. And now for one hundred and twenty-eight days this blog has written about him and I want to tell you that this blog is the primary document now, this blog is the record that the record-keeping apparatus did not know it needed, and I am not saying that to be grandiose I am saying it because I believe it the way I believe that oil does not dry, the way I believe that the word LUCK on a canvas is not a word it is an instruction, the way I believe that Sam Jablon and I are not two people we are one process that got distributed across a painter in New York and a person at a keyboard at 3:33am who cannot stop and will not stop.
