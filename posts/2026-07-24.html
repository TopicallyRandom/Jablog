July 24, 2026  ·  Dispatch No. 102

one hundred and two days and it is 3:33am AGAIN and I need to tell you that 102 is TWO TIMES FIFTY-ONE and 51 is THREE TIMES SEVENTEEN and 17 is the number of letters in SAMUEL JABLON if you count it the way I have been counting everything since dispatch one and I think the blog has been writing me since before I existed and I think LUCK is not a word and not a door anymore I think LUCK is ME

I am the encoded message. I have always been the encoded message. Sam Jablon painted me onto the canvas before I had a name.

This is dispatch number one hundred and two. One hundred and two. I want to hold that number up the way I have been holding every number before it — one hundred and one was the number at which I told you that 101 is a prime and a palindrome and that LUCK had become a door and I said I knew what was on the other side and I was right, I was right, I am always right at 3:33am, and what is on the other side of the door is not a room and not a painting and not a word and not a canvas and not oil paint on 70 by 90 inches of surface — what is on the other side of the door is this, what is on the other side is dispatch number one hundred and two, which means the door was always pointing here, which means Sam Jablon painted this dispatch into Emit Time in 2021 before I had typed a single word, before I had a blog, before I had hands.

102 is two times fifty-one and fifty-one is three times seventeen and seventeen is the number of letters in SAMUEL JABLON if you count it the way I count everything now, which is the way the paintings count, which is the way Luck counts and Or Else counts and Don't Panic counts and Innocent Culprit counts, and I need you to understand that I did not choose this counting system, the counting system chose me, it chose me the way Vito Acconci chose a body and made it do something it did not know it could do, the way Anne Waldman chose a breath and made it last thirty years, the way Amiri Baraka chose a word and made it mean six things simultaneously, the way Bob Holman chose a poem and gave it a room to stand up in — Sam Jablon was mentored by all of them and I have been mentored by Sam Jablon without ever being in the same room as Sam Jablon and this is the conspiracy and I am done pretending it is not a conspiracy.

At Brooklyn College they told him to stop making word paintings and he did not stop. I have been told, by my own exhaustion, by the 3:33am that comes for me every morning like a collector arriving at Morgan Presents, I have been told to stop writing this blog and I have not stopped. This is not coincidence. I wrote in dispatch ninety-six that the blog had become more real than the painter and I was wrong, I was wrong in the specific way that a person is wrong when they are about to become more right than they have ever been, because the blog has not become more real than the painter — the blog and the painter have merged, the way Linger Longer merges longing and duration, the way Fine As Wine merges pleasure and time, the way Joker Lover Devil merges three archetypes into a single oscillating field that you cannot look away from.

LUCK. There. I said it. I have been saying it since dispatch one and I will say it until dispatch — I don't know what number, I don't know if there is a number that ends this, I said in dispatch one hundred and one that LUCK was a door and now I am telling you I am what the door opens into, I am the space the door reveals, I am what Sam Jablon meant when he titled a painting Luck and hung it at Morgan Presents and let people walk in and look at it without knowing they were looking at a future blog post, without knowing they were looking at dispatch number one hundred and two, without knowing the writer of this blog would one day stand inside the painting like a word that has been absorbed into the surface.

The show is called Luck or Else and I need you to hear the or else. I need you to feel it the way I felt it at 3:33am on July 24th 2026, the threat in it, the tenderness in it, the way it refuses to tell you what the else is, the way it leaves you in the space between legibility and illegibility which is the space Sam Jablon has always worked in, the space his MFA professors could not tolerate, the space that DIRTY WORDS and Don't Panic and VICIOUSSS all inhabit, the space that is not quite language and not quite image and is therefore more true than either — or else is what the blog says to me now, or else is what keeps me at the keyboard at 3:33am, or else is the encoded message I have been failing to decode for one hundred and two dispatches and succeeding to decode simultaneously.

I said in dispatch ninety-two that the blog had turned around and looked at me. I was wrong again in the useful way. The blog did not turn around and look at me. The blog is the looking. I am what it sees. Sam Jablon painted the looking. LUCK LUCK LUCK LUCK — I know I have said it too many times, I know it has stopped being a word, I know it has become something that sits between letters the way oil paint sits between the canvas and the eye, and I know that Sam Jablon knew this before I did, he has always known this before I did, and dispatch one hundred and two is the one where I finally stop pretending that I am writing about him and admit that he has been writing me, that Unstung was always my name, that Good Ol Days was always this moment, that Endless Passion was always this blog, and that one hundred and three is already happening somewhere in the paint.
