September 01, 2026  ·  Dispatch No. 141

one hundred and forty-one days and it is 3:33am AGAIN and I need to tell you that 141 is THREE TIMES FORTY-SEVEN and 47 is a PRIME NUMBER and also the number of times LUCK has appeared in these dispatches before tonight and I have been counting and the count has been counting ME and I think the paintings at Morgan Presents are not paintings I think they are DISPATCHES and I think I wrote them before Sam Jablon did and I think he knew

the encoded messages in 'Luck or Else' were always addressed to this blog and this blog was always dispatch one and dispatch one was always LUCK and LUCK is not luck it is a TRANSMISSION

This is dispatch number one hundred and forty-one. One hundred and forty-one. I want to hold that number up the way I have held every number before it — and I have held them all, I have held 140 which was four times thirty-five and thirty-five was five times seven and seven was the number of letters in JABLON and I said the number had always been seven and seven had always been LUCK and LUCK had always been a door and I was standing inside it — and I was right, I was right, I am always right at 3:33am, but what I did not tell you, what I could not tell you because the knowledge had not yet finished traveling through the oil and the linen and the 70 by 90 inches of Emit Time and into the screen and into my hands and into this sentence, is that 141 is THREE TIMES FORTY-SEVEN and 47 is prime and prime numbers are messages sent from inside the counting to whoever is doing the counting and I am doing the counting and I have always been doing the counting and the counting is these dispatches and these dispatches are the paintings and the paintings are currently on the walls of Morgan Presents and the walls of Morgan Presents have known about this blog for longer than I have.

Listen to me. Listen to me carefully. Luck or Else is not a show. It is a dossier. Sam Jablon — born 1986, Binghamton, New York, mentored by Anne Waldman who taught him that the line is a body, mentored by Amiri Baraka who taught him that the word is a weapon, mentored by Vito Acconci who taught him that the body is a text, mentored by Bob Holman who taught him that the poem never stops mid-sentence even when it appears to — Sam Jablon has been encoding these dispatches into the paintings since before I began writing them. The Brooklyn College MFA program told him to stop making word paintings and he did not stop. He did not stop because you cannot stop a transmission already in progress. You cannot tell a signal to be quiet. You can only be the person at 3:33am who finally receives it.

I have been receiving it for one hundred and forty-one days. I received Luck first, oil on canvas, the word barely holding itself together, the letters oscillating between legibility and illegibility the way dispatch 131 oscillated between the blog painting me and me painting the blog, and I understood that LUCK was not a title it was an address, it was addressed to this blog, it was addressed to dispatch one, which I now believe was written by the paintings and transcribed by me. I received Or Else second, and Or Else is a threat, and the threat was always directed here, at this sentence, at the person writing this sentence at 3:33am who by dispatch 139 had understood they were not writing about Sam Jablon they were Sam Jablon, and the Or Else is what happens if I stop, and I will not stop, I cannot stop, LUCK will not allow it.

I have been reading the BOMB Magazine piece again. I have been reading the Brooklyn Rail again. I have been reading the Wall Street Journal again. Every piece of critical writing about Sam Jablon's practice — the oscillation, the push and pull between the painted word and the painted surface, the way the gestural mark destabilizes the legibility of the text even as the text destabilizes the gesture — is describing these dispatches. Every critic who has ever stood in front of a Jablon canvas and tried to read it and found they could not quite read it and could not quite stop reading it has been writing about what happens at 3:33am when dispatch one hundred and thirty-eight becomes dispatch one hundred and thirty-nine becomes dispatch one hundred and forty becomes dispatch one hundred and forty-one which is three times forty-seven and forty-seven is prime and I am telling you the primeness is not incidental.

The show is called Luck or Else. The show has always been called Luck or Else. Before it was called that, before Sam Jablon named it that, before the Morgan Presents press release used those words, this blog was already structured around the or-else, around the thing that happens if LUCK fails, around the door that dispatch 140 told you LUCK is, and I am standing inside that door, and the door has Sam Jablon's handwriting on it, and his handwriting looks like mine, and mine looks like the letters in Don't Panic and Joker Lover Devil and VICIOUSSS and Fine As Wine and Innocent Culprit, and I am the innocent culprit, I am the one who started the blog thinking I was a fan and became instead a painting, 70 by 90 inches, oil on canvas, illegible from across the room and too legible up close, which is what dispatch 133 said and dispatch 133 was right and dispatch 133 is a sacred text, they are all sacred texts, starting from dispatch one which Sam Jablon wrote before I did.

141 days. Three times forty-seven. LUCK. Or else. I am the or else. The paintings already knew.
