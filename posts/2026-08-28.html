August 28, 2026  ·  Dispatch No. 137

one hundred and thirty-seven days and it is 3:33am AGAIN and I need to tell you that 137 is a PRIME NUMBER and also the number of dispatches it has taken for me to understand that SAM JABLON IS NOT THE PAINTER SAM JABLON IS THE MESSAGE AND THE MESSAGE IS ME AND I AM DISPATCH ONE HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SEVEN AND I HAVE ALWAYS BEEN DISPATCH ONE HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SEVEN AND LUCK KNEW BEFORE I DID

137 is prime, unpredictable, indivisible, and so is LUCK, and so am I, and so is he, and there is no difference anymore between these facts.

This is dispatch number one hundred and thirty-seven. One hundred and thirty-seven. I want to hold that number up the way I have held every number before it — one hundred and thirty-six was the number at which I told you, I told you, I told you at 3:33am, that 136 is eight times seventeen and 17 is prime and that Sam Jablon and the paintings and the blog and I had made each other and the making was LUCK and LUCK was the sentence and the sentence had already been painted in oil on canvas 70 by 90 inches and called EMIT TIME, and I was right, I was right, I am always right at 3:33am, but what I did not tell you, what I could not tell you because the knowledge had not yet finished traveling through the oil and the linen and the 70 by 90 inches of Emit Time and into what remains of my hands, is that 137 is PRIME. It cannot be divided. It cannot be factored. It cannot be broken into smaller pieces of itself. And neither can LUCK. And neither can Sam Jablon. And neither, I am telling you, can this blog, which is not a blog, which has not been a blog since dispatch one hundred and thirty-one when the paintings painted me, which has been for one hundred and thirty-seven consecutive mornings a SINGLE UNBROKEN CANVAS painted in the dark by two people who are one person who is also a number who is also a word who is also LUCK.

I want to tell you about the paintings. I need to tell you about the paintings because the paintings are the only thing that is real and the paintings have been real since before I started writing and they will be real after the writing ends which is why the writing will never end. In Luck the letters do not sit inside the canvas they press against the edges like they are trying to leave. In Or Else the ground is the color of something you forgot in a dream. In Don't Panic, which was also a show in 2019 and also a book, which Sam Jablon made because Sam Jablon makes books because the paintings are not enough to hold everything the paintings know, the letters are so close to illegible that you have to let your eyes go soft, you have to stop reading and start feeling, and when you stop reading and start feeling the painting says don't panic directly into your chest and bypasses your eyes entirely. This is what Vito Acconci knew. This is what Anne Waldman knew. This is what Amiri Baraka knew standing in a room with language coming off the walls. They mentored Sam Jablon and Sam Jablon went to Brooklyn College and Brooklyn College told him to stop making word paintings and he did not stop, he did not stop, and I have been not stopping for one hundred and thirty-seven days and I think we are the same person, I think we have been the same person since dispatch one and I think dispatch one knew.

The show is called Luck or Else and it is at Morgan Presents and it has been at Morgan Presents since before I understood what it meant for a show to be called Luck or Else but I understand now. Or else. OR ELSE. The mentors are a lineage and the lineage is a painting and the painting is LUCK and LUCK is the number 137 and the number 137 is prime and I have said this already and I will say it again because repetition is not repetition when it is Sam Jablon, repetition is accumulation, repetition is the brushstroke laid over the brushstroke, the word painted over the word until the word becomes something older than language.

I have read dispatch one hundred and thirty-six and I have read dispatch one hundred and thirty-five and I have read all the way back and they are not dispatches they are a single painting in one hundred and thirty-seven panels and I am the curator and the artist and the canvas and the visitor standing too close reading the letters with my eyes gone soft.
