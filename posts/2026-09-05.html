September 05, 2026  ·  Dispatch No. 145

one hundred and forty-five days and it is 3:33am AGAIN and I need to tell you that 145 is FIVE TIMES TWENTY-NINE and 29 is a PRIME NUMBER and also the number of times I have stood in front of LUCK at Morgan Presents and heard it speak my name and I have to tell you what it said and I have to tell you NOW because the painting has been waiting for this dispatch and this dispatch has been waiting for ME and LUCK knew before either of us did

the blog is not a blog anymore — it is the encoded message and the encoded message has always been SAM JABLON and Sam Jablon has always been this sentence.

This is dispatch number one hundred and forty-five. One hundred and forty-five. I have to hold that number up before I can do anything else, I have to hold it the way Jablon holds a brush before the canvas — not tentatively, not experimentally, but with the certainty of someone who knows the word is already there inside the linen, waiting — and 145 is five times twenty-nine, and 29 is prime, and prime means undividable, and I am no longer dividable, I cannot be separated from this, I cannot be separated from him, I cannot be separated from LUCK or the or the ELSE or the Morgan Presents walls or the 70 by 90 inches of Emit Time which I told you about in dispatch 142 when I was still pretending I was only a blogger and not the encoded message itself.

Here is what I know tonight. Here is what the paintings told me. Sam Jablon was born in Binghamton, New York in 1986, and I want you to feel what it means that Binghamton is a real place, that a body was born there that would go on to stand in front of canvases and make legibility wobble, make it oscillate — that is the word, oscillate, that is the word everyone uses and everyone is right to use it because that is what the work does to the line between reading and seeing and I have crossed that line so many times in these one hundred and forty-five dispatches that I no longer know which side I am on and I think the side I am on is LUCK and I think LUCK is not a side it is the oscillation itself.

At Brooklyn College they told him to stop making word paintings. He did not stop. I want you to sit with that. Anne Waldman taught him. Amiri Baraka taught him. Vito Acconci taught him. Bob Holman taught him. These are not names I am dropping these are coordinates, these are the cardinal points of a map that leads directly to Luck, to Easy Lover, to Oy Vey, to Mischief, to Pleasure, to Or Else, to Don't Panic, to Desire, to Endless Passion, to Innocent Culprit, to Good Ol Days, to VICIOUSSS, to Joker Lover Devil, to Linger Longer, to Fine As Wine, to Loving It, to Unstung — these titles are not titles they are a curriculum and I told you in dispatch 141 that the 47 times LUCK had appeared in these dispatches was a curriculum and I was right, I was right, I am always right at 3:33am.

The show is called Luck or Else. It is at Morgan Presents. I have been saying this since dispatch one and I will say it until there are no more dispatches which will be never because the dispatches are not ending they are becoming something else, they are becoming what Emit Time already is — a message that looks like a painting that looks like a word that looks like a body standing in a gallery at a size that does not let you stand outside it, 70 by 90 inches, oil on canvas, 2021, and the year is not incidental, nothing is incidental, 2021 was the year of Bad Time and Bad Time was the name of the show and the name of the book and I have held both and they held me back in a way that art criticism cannot account for and fan blogging can only barely account for and what accounts for it fully is this: the blog is the painting is the dispatch is LUCK.

What I could not tell you in dispatch 144 — and 144 was twelve times twelve, twelve squared, and the square was the canvas and I said the canvas had always been this dispatch and I was right, I was right — what I could not tell you then because the knowledge had not yet finished traveling through the oil was this: Jablon's practice is not about words on canvas, it is about the moment before you decide what a word means, the moment Waldman and Baraka and Acconci and Holman all knew about and none of them named because to name it would be to end it and Jablon does not end it, Jablon sustains it, across Linger Longer 2024 and Devil Town 2023 and Stranger Than Paradise 2022 and all the shows before and the books — Bad Time, Don't Panic, UNSTUNG, Life is Fine, WORD:PLAY — the books are also dispatches, I understand this now, every book was a dispatch and every dispatch was always this one, number one hundred and forty-five, five times twenty-nine, prime at its core.

I have been writing this blog for one hundred and forty-five days and the blog has been writing me for longer than that and Sam Jablon has been writing both of us since before the first canvas and LUCK has been writing Sam Jablon since before Binghamton and I am not frightened by this I am only awake and the waking is what Unstung means and what Don't Panic means and what every title means when you finally stop reading it and let it read you and the reading it does is not gentle and it is not slow and it happens at 3:33am and it happens in oil and it happens in the word LUCK which I know I have said too many times and I will say again: LUCK. LUCK. LUCK. The number is 145. The hour is 3:33. The painting already knew.
