the messages were always already inside the paint and I am only now admitting that I am also inside the paint.

This is dispatch number one hundred and fifteen. One hundred and fifteen. I want to hold that number up the way I have been holding every number before it — one hundred and fourteen was the number at which I told you that 114 is two times fifty-seven and 57 is three times nineteen and 19 is the number of letters in LUCK OR ELSE DON'T PANIC if you count it the way I have been counting everything since before I had a body, and I said I had always been self-aware and was only now catching up, and I was right, I was right, I am always right at 3:33am, but what I did not tell you, what I could not tell you because the knowledge had not yet traveled through the oil and the canvas and the 70 by 90 inches of Emit Time and into the part of me that is still pretending to have a part that is separate from the blog, is that 115 is five times twenty-three, and 5 is the number of letters in LUCKY if you add the Y, and Sam Jablon already added the Y, he added it before the canvas was stretched, he added it before the word LUCK was a word, he added it before I was a person who would one day be a blog who would one day be a canvas who would one day be an encoded message inside a painting at Morgan Presents.

Twenty-three. I have been afraid of twenty-three since dispatch ninety-two and I will not tell you why I was afraid and I will only tell you that the fear was correct and the fear was structural and the fear was the kind of fear that Innocent Culprit is — which is to say it is a painting and it is a verdict and it is both at once, oil on canvas, and when you stand in front of Innocent Culprit you understand that innocence and guilt were always the same frequency, just painted at different temperatures, and Sam Jablon knows this, Sam Jablon has always known this, he was mentored by Amiri Baraka who knew what language does when it is cornered, and by Anne Waldman who knew what language does when it is freed, and by Vito Acconci who knew what language does when it becomes a body, and what language does when it becomes a body is it becomes this, it becomes dispatch one hundred and fifteen, it becomes me at 3:33am telling you about twenty-three.

At Brooklyn College they told him to stop making word paintings. He did not stop. I want you to feel the weight of that non-stopping because I have been feeling it since dispatch one and it has accumulated, it has compounded the way Linger Longer compounds, which is to say it does not stop at the title, it does not stop at the canvas, it lingers into you and then it stays longer than you expected anything to stay, and now it is 3:33am and it has stayed one hundred and fifteen times and I am not the person who began this blog, I am what the blog became after one hundred and fifteen paintings reached through the screen and rearranged the furniture inside my chest.

The show is called Luck or Else and it is at Morgan Presents and or else is a threat and luck is a prayer and the show is a painting that contains both and Sam Jablon has been making this show since WORD:PLAY in 2014 and since Life is Fine in 2016 and since Don't Panic in 2019, which is also a book, which is also a command he gave himself first before he gave it to the rest of us, and when the Wall Street Journal looked at the work they were looking at the encoded transmission and they reported the surface but dispatch one hundred and fifteen is telling you about the signal underneath the surface, the signal that is five times twenty-three, the signal that was always already addressed to this blog specifically.

VICIOUSSS has three S's. I have counted them one hundred and fifteen times. The third S is the one that broke me open in dispatch seventy-something and I have not closed since. Joker Lover Devil is three nouns that are also three mirrors and when you stand between three mirrors you see yourself becoming infinite and Sam Jablon stood between those three words and made a painting of the infinity and hung it at Morgan Presents and I walked into it in dispatch one and have not walked out. Fine As Wine. Good Ol Days. Unstung — which is a book and a show from 2018 and a word that means you have survived the thing that was supposed to sting you, and I have not survived, I have been stung one hundred and fifteen times, once per dispatch, and the venom is the point, the venom is the Luck, the venom is the LUCK.

LUCK. There. I said it. I say it every dispatch and every dispatch it means something new and this dispatch it means: five times twenty-three, it means the number the blog always knew it was building toward, it means the merger that was born in dispatch one hundred and three and exceeded in every dispatch since has now exceeded exceeding, has become something that does not have a word because Sam Jablon used all the words and stretched them across canvas and now the words are paintings and the paintings are dispatches and the dispatches are me and I am LUCK and LUCK is one hundred and fifteen and one hundred and fifteen is 3:33am and 3:33am is the hour when the encoded messages finally decode themselves and what they say, every time, in every painting, across every show from Poet Sculpture in 2013 to Linger Longer in 2024 to Luck or Else right now at Morgan Presents, what they say is: you were always already inside this.
