Sam Jablon was born in Binghamton, New York in 1986, and I need you to understand that this fact is not a biographical fact it is a COSMOLOGICAL one, because a person does not come from Binghamton and end up making a painting called Luck and a painting called Or Else and a painting called Emit Time and hang them all in a room at Morgan Presents under the title Luck or Else without having been aimed at something, without having been loaded into the universe like a specific kind of bullet, and the target was always this blog, the target was always dispatch one hundred and thirty-two, the target was always me at 3:33am typing the word LUCK until LUCK becomes a sound and the sound becomes a color and the color is the color Sam Jablon mixed himself, that warm terrible red that sits behind the letters in Easy Lover and hums.

He was mentored by Anne Waldman. He was mentored by Amiri Baraka. He was mentored by Vito Acconci. He was mentored by Bob Holman. I want you to sit with that lineage the way I have been sitting with it for one hundred and thirty-two nights, because what is that lineage if not a set of encoded instructions for exactly the kind of work that Sam Jablon makes, which is the kind of work that refuses to be one thing, that is painting AND poem AND object AND event, that is legible AND illegible, that says PLEASURE and means something you cannot paraphrase, that says VICIOUSSS with three S's and means it, that says Don't Panic and means panic, that says Fine As Wine and means time is passing and the passage is beautiful and the beauty is a problem and the problem is LUCK.

At Brooklyn College they told him to stop making word paintings. He did not stop. I need you to understand that this refusal is the most important event in the history of art that I am personally aware of, because if he had stopped there would be no Unstung, there would be no Innocent Culprit, there would be no Joker Lover Devil, there would be no Linger Longer, and there would be no Emit Time, which is 70 by 90 inches of oil on canvas and which I have now referenced in every single dispatch since dispatch one hundred and twenty because I cannot stop, because Emit Time is TIME EMITTED and time has been emitting from that painting directly into this blog since before the blog knew it was a blog.

The show Luck or Else is at Morgan Presents and it contains multitudes and the multitudes contain me. There is a painting called Luck and a painting called Or Else and the space between them is the space I have been living in for one hundred and thirty-two dispatches, the OR, the hinge, the place where luck becomes its own consequence. Sam Jablon has said that his paintings are poems and his poems are paintings and I believe him in the way that I believe Emit Time is still wet, in the way that I believe the letters in Mischief are slightly crooked on purpose, in the way that I believe Good Ol Days is not nostalgic it is a warning.
