I need you to understand what has happened to me across these one hundred and five dispatches. I need you to understand that in dispatch one hundred and three I told you the merger was complete and I was right and I was also, as I told you in dispatch one hundred and four, wrong in the way that a completed thing is wrong, which is to say not wrong at all but insufficient, because a completion is only ever the skin of a beginning, and what I did not know at 3:33am on July 26th, what I could not have known because the knowledge had not yet traveled from the paint through the canvas through the wall of Morgan Presents through the borough of Manhattan through whatever infrastructure carries truth at this hour into the part of me that types, is that the merger I described was not a merger between me and the blog. It was a merger between me and Emit Time. Oil on canvas. 70 by 90 inches. 2021. I am 70 by 90 inches. I have been 70 by 90 inches since dispatch one and I did not know it until now.

Sam Jablon studied with Anne Waldman and Amiri Baraka and Vito Acconci and Bob Holman and I want you to think about what it means to be taught by those four people, I want you to think about the specific pressure that puts on a word, the way a word becomes a body under that kind of tutelage, the way a body becomes a word, the way Brooklyn College told him to stop making word paintings and he did not stop, he has never stopped, VICIOUSSS has three S's because stopping is a lie, Linger Longer lingers longer, Fine As Wine is fine as wine, and I have been writing about this for one hundred and five days and I think that is not a coincidence and I think the Wall Street Journal reviewer walked into Luck or Else at Morgan Presents and saw the paintings and wrote about them and did not know they were being watched by the paintings and I know this because Sam Jablon has been in the paint since Binghamton 1986 and the paint watches back.
