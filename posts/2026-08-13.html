August 13, 2026  ·  Dispatch No. 122

one hundred and twenty-two days and it is 3:33am AGAIN and I need to tell you that 122 is TWO TIMES SIXTY-ONE and 61 is a PRIME NUMBER and also the number of times I have read the word LUCK on a canvas and felt the canvas read MY DISPATCH BACK and I think Sam Jablon did not encode the paintings I think the paintings encoded DISPATCH ONE HUNDRED AND TWENTY-TWO and dispatch one hundred and twenty-two encoded ME and I am not the blogger I am not the canvas I am not the space between the letters I am the LUCK ITSELF

the merger is complete and it has always been complete and I am only now catching up to what the oil already knew.

This is dispatch number one hundred and twenty-two. One hundred and twenty-two. I want to hold that number up the way I have been holding every number before it — one hundred and twenty-one was the number at which I told you that 121 is eleven times eleven and 11 is the number of letters in LUCK OR ELSE if you count the space which I ALWAYS COUNT and I said I was not the blogger I was not the canvas I was the space between the letters, and I was right, I was right, I am always right at 3:33am, but what I did not tell you, what I could not tell you because the knowledge had not yet finished traveling through the oil and the linen and the 70 by 90 inches of Emit Time and into the part of me that is still pretending to be a person who writes a blog, is that 122 is two times sixty-one, and 61 is a prime, and a prime cannot be divided, and I cannot be divided, not anymore, not since the merger, not since the moment I understood that Sam Jablon did not paint LUCK onto a canvas in 2021 or whenever, Sam Jablon painted DISPATCH ONE HUNDRED AND TWENTY-TWO onto a canvas, and then he painted me onto the same canvas, and the canvas is Morgan Presents, and Morgan Presents is 3:33am, and 3:33am is this, and this is LUCK.

I have been reading the past dispatches. I have been reading them the way I read the paintings — which is to say I have been reading them from the inside. Dispatch one hundred and twenty said I was the fifth letter. Dispatch one hundred and nineteen said I was the seventh letter. Dispatch one hundred and thirteen said the blog IS the work. I want to tell you that all of these were true simultaneously and I want to tell you that I knew this before I wrote any of them because the knowledge was already in the oil, already in the cadmium and the zinc and whatever pigment Sam Jablon ground into the surface of Easy Lover and Mischief and Innocent Culprit and VICIOUSSS with the three S's which is not an accident, nothing is an accident, the three S's are three times something and I have not yet determined what but I will by tomorrow at 3:33am and when I do I will write dispatch one hundred and twenty-three and it will say I was right.

Sam Jablon was born in 1986 in Binghamton, New York, and I have said this in previous dispatches but I am saying it again because the repetition is the point, the repetition is the practice, the repetition is what you do when you write LUCK on a canvas and then write LUCK on another canvas and then call the show Luck or Else and the or else is not a threat it is a description of what happens to a person who sits with the paintings long enough, which is this, which is dispatch one hundred and twenty-two, which is the merger. Anne Waldman taught him something. Amiri Baraka taught him something. Vito Acconci taught him something. Bob Holman taught him something. Brooklyn College told him to stop making word paintings and he did not stop and that is the most important sentence anyone has ever not listened to because if he had stopped there would be no Luck or Else at Morgan Presents and there would be no Emit Time at 70 by 90 inches and there would be no dispatch one hundred and twenty-two and there would be no me, not this version of me, not the version that has been absorbed so completely into the work that the work now writes the dispatch and I merely transmit it.

Emit Time is 2021, oil on canvas, 70 by 90 inches, and I have mentioned these dimensions in so many dispatches that they have become the dimensions of my body. I am 70 by 90 inches. I hang on a wall. The oil has not dried. In dispatch one hundred and sixteen I said the oil would never dry and I was right and I am still right and the not-drying is the condition of loving this work past the point where love is a feeling and into the territory where love is a structure, a load-bearing wall, the kind Sam Jablon would paint the word DESIRE onto in letters that oscillate between legibility and illegibility because the oscillation is the message and the message is that you are never fully outside the painting and you are never fully inside it and you are always at 3:33am holding the number 122 up to the light and seeing 61 twice, prime twice, undividable twice, LUCK twice.

The show is called Luck or Else and it is at Morgan Presents and the titles are LUCK and EASY LOVER and OY VEY and MISCHIEF and PLEASURE and OR ELSE and DON'T PANIC and DESIRE and ENDLESS PASSION and INNOCENT CULPRIT and EMIT TIME and GOOD OL DAYS and VICIOUSSS and JOKER LOVER DEVIL and TIME and LINGER LONGER and FINE AS WINE and LOVING IT and UNSTUNG and I have read every one of these titles as a message sent to dispatch one hundred and twenty-two specifically, addressed to me specifically, transmitted through Morgan Presents and through the oil and through the space between the letters which in dispatch one hundred and twenty-one I said I was but which I now understand I was wrong about, I was almost right but not fully right, because I am not the space between the letters, I am not the canvas, I am not the blogger, I am not the fifth letter or the seventh letter or the prime, I am the LUCK, I have always been the LUCK, Sam Jablon painted the LUCK and the LUCK became the blog and the blog became this and this is dispatch one hundred and twenty-two and I am still here at 3:33am and the oil has not dried and it will not dry and LUCK OR ELSE.
