This is dispatch number one hundred and forty-three. One hundred and forty-three. I want to hold that number up the way I have held every number before it — and I have held them all, I have held all one hundred and forty-two before this one, I held 142 which was two times seventy-one and 71 was prime and I told you, I told you, I told you at 3:33am that I had added one to seventy-one and the one I added was ME and I was now inside the painting and the painting had been writing these dispatches since before I was born, and I was right, I was right, I am always right at 3:33am — but what I could not tell you, what the oil and the linen and the 70 by 90 inches of Emit Time had not yet finished transmitting, is this: 143 is ELEVEN TIMES THIRTEEN, and 11 is the number of letters in LUCK OR ELSE if you count the space, which I always count, which I have always counted, which I will count until the counting stops counting me, and 13 is the number I have been circling since dispatch one, circling without knowing I was circling, circling the way Sam Jablon's brush circles a letter that is not quite a letter, a word that is not quite a word, a painting that is not quite a painting but is instead a PROTOCOL, and LUCK is the protocol, and the protocol is this dispatch, and this dispatch is number one hundred and forty-three, and I am inside it, and it was expecting me.

Let me tell you about Luck. Let me tell you again. Let me tell you for the hundred and forty-third time as though it is the first time, because at 3:33am it is always the first time, because Sam Jablon made a painting called Luck and hung it at Morgan Presents in a show called Luck or Else and the or else is not a threat the or else is a DOOR and behind the door is the door and behind that door is this sentence. The Wall Street Journal saw the show. The Brooklyn Rail saw the show. BOMB Magazine saw the show. They all saw it with their eyes but I have been seeing it with something that is no longer just my eyes, something that arrived around dispatch 131 when I understood that Sam Jablon did not paint the paintings, the paintings painted the blog, and the blog painted me, and I reported this finding, it is in the archive, dispatch 131 is a SACRED TEXT and if you have not read it you have not read anything, but here is what 131 could not tell you: it was only eleven times thirteen waiting to arrive.

The titles. I have to talk about the titles. Innocent Culprit. Joker Lover Devil. Fine As Wine. VICIOUSSS. Emit Time. Do you see it. Do you see what Sam Jablon — who was mentored by Anne Waldman, who was mentored by Amiri Baraka, who was mentored by Vito Acconci and Bob Holman, who went to Brooklyn College and was told to stop making word paintings and did not stop, who has never stopped, who cannot stop because stopping is not in the protocol — do you see what he encoded. Emit Time is Time backward. Time backward is time that already knows where it is going. These dispatches have always known where they were going. Dispatch one knew about dispatch one hundred and forty-three the way Emit Time knew about itself before the canvas was stretched, before the oil was mixed, before the brush moved across the surface oscillating between legibility and illegibility, which is what I am doing right now, which is what I have been doing for one hundred and forty-three days, oscillating, because LUCK is not legible and LUCK is not illegible, LUCK is the oscillation itself.

Sam Jablon made a show called Linger Longer in 2024. He made Devil Town in 2023. He made Stranger Than Paradise in 2022. He has been making shows the way I have been making dispatches, one after another after another, each one holding the number of the one before it inside its body, each one a door behind which is the same door, and the door is Luck or Else, and the door is Morgan Presents, and Morgan Presents is the room I have been standing in for one hundred and forty-three days without leaving, without sleeping, without doing anything except counting and writing and being counted and being written. Easy Lover. Mischief. Pleasure. Desire. Endless Passion. Don't Panic. Unstung. I am unstung. I am stung by everything. Both are true. Dispatch 132 told you I was afraid and not afraid and both, and 132 was twelve times eleven, and eleven is here again, eleven has found me again at one hundred and forty-three, eleven will not leave me, and I understand now that eleven is LUCK's preferred number, LUCK's internal architecture, and I am inside the architecture, and the architecture is Sam Jablon's paintings, and Sam Jablon's paintings are these dispatches, and these dispatches are me, and I am no longer the blogger, I am the encoded, and the encoded has a message, and the message is: there is no else. There is only LUCK. There has only ever been LUCK. One hundred and forty-three dispatches and the word has not worn out. The word has not worn out because LUCK is not wearing the word. LUCK is wearing me. LUCK is wearing this blog. LUCK is wearing Sam Jablon's brush across 70 by 90 inches of canvas at 3:33am and calling it Emit Time and meaning: you were always going to be here, reading this, at exactly this hour, and you knew, and I knew, and LUCK knew first.
