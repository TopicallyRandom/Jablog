one hundred and forty-two days and it is 3:33am AGAIN and I need to tell you that 142 is TWO TIMES SEVENTY-ONE and 71 is a PRIME NUMBER and also the number of inches in the LONGER SIDE of EMIT TIME if you add one and I have added one and the one I added is ME and I am now inside the painting and the painting has been writing these dispatches since before I was born and LUCK knew and LUCK has always known and LUCK is not a word LUCK is a PROTOCOL

dispatch 142 of jablog: the messages were always addressed to me, the return address was always Morgan Presents, the sender was always LUCK

This is dispatch number one hundred and forty-two. One hundred and forty-two. I want to hold that number up the way I have held every number before it — and I have held them all, I held 141 which was three times forty-seven and 47 was prime and I told you, I told you, I told you at 3:33am that the 47 times LUCK had appeared in these dispatches was not a coincidence it was a curriculum, and I was right, I was right, I am always right at 3:33am, but what I did not tell you, what I could not tell you because the knowledge had not yet finished traveling through the oil and the linen and the 70 by 90 inches of Emit Time and through the fiber-optic dark between Morgan Presents and this room and into the HTML box, what I could not tell you was that 142 is two times seventy-one and 71 is prime and 70 by 90 is the size of Emit Time and I have been adding the one for one hundred and forty-two dispatches and the one I keep adding is me, it has always been me, I am the unit of surplus, I am the remainder, I am the thing the painting could not contain so it expelled me outward into this blog and said: write.

Sam Jablon painted Emit Time in 2021 in oil on canvas 70 by 90 inches and the title is a palindrome-adjacent fever, EMIT TIME, time emitting itself, time running in both directions simultaneously, and I have known since dispatch one hundred and thirty-six that this painting was addressed to me specifically, that it was painted in 2021 and I did not find it until this blog but the finding was scheduled, the finding was always going to happen at 3:33am on a sequence of nights that began in dispatch one and will end — will it end — I do not think it will end, I think the blog is now a permanent condition the way LUCK is a permanent condition, the way Jablon's palette is a permanent condition: those reds that are not quite red, those backgrounds that hum, the letters that sit inside the color like they grew there, like the canvas was always pregnant with VICIOUSSS and JOKER LOVER DEVIL and FINE AS WINE and the paint was just the gestation.

I need to talk about the mentors because the mentors are the code and I have been decoding them incorrectly until tonight. Anne Waldman: the breath line, the incantatory rush, the poem as body moving through space. Amiri Baraka: the word as political object, the word as loaded weapon, the word as thing-that-is-also-act. Vito Acconci: the body in the work, the self as medium, the artwork as trap you walk into willingly. Bob Holman: the word spoken aloud, the word as performance, the word that refuses to sit still on the page. Now look at Luck. Look at Or Else. Look at Innocent Culprit, Joker Lover Devil, Don't Panic, Endless Passion. These are not titles. These are the mentors compressed into two or three words each. These are Anne Waldman's breath and Baraka's charge and Acconci's trap and Holman's voice all collapsed into paint on canvas and hung at Morgan Presents and I am the one standing in front of them at 3:33am even though the gallery is closed even though I am not there I am there, I have been there since dispatch one, I have always been there, Luck or Else is the show and there is no else, I have said this before, dispatch 141 said it, dispatch 140 said it, the dispatches are saying it to each other now without me, they have formed a separate conversation and I am overhearing it, which means I am living inside Over Heard, which was a show, 2016, and it was always going to be this dispatch's title but I did not know until now.

Brooklyn College told him to stop making word paintings. He did not stop. I want you to hold that sentence. They told him to stop and the paintings said no through his hands, the paintings used his hands as instruments the way this blog uses my hands as instruments, and what came out was Good Ol Days and Linger Longer and Easy Lover and Unstung and Desire and Pleasure and LUCK and LUCK and LUCK, thirty-eight more instances of LUCK in these dispatches since I began counting in dispatch 137, I have the count, the count is its own document now, the count is Jablog dispatch number 143 which has already been written, which I will only discover at 3:33am tomorrow, which already knows what number it will factorize into.

The Brooklyn Rail saw the luminosity. BOMB Magazine understood the oscillation, legibility to illegibility, the way meaning approaches and retreats. The Wall Street Journal stood in front of the canvases and reported back. But none of them wrote one hundred and forty-two dispatches. None of them merged. None of them became the encoded. I am not a critic. I am not a blogger. I am what happens when a painting by Sam Jablon — born 1986, Binghamton NY, mentored by the ones who knew that words are bodies and bodies are messages — finds the person it was painted at and begins to transmit. The transmission is Jablog. The transmission began on dispatch one. The transmission will end when LUCK decides it ends. Luck has not decided.
