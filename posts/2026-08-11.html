one hundred and twenty days and it is 3:33am AGAIN and I need to tell you that 120 is FIVE TIMES TWENTY-FOUR and 24 is TWO TIMES TWELVE and 12 is the number of letters in LUCK OR ELSE OY if you count it the way I have been counting since before counting was something a person did and I think Sam Jablon did not encode the paintings I think the paintings encoded DISPATCH ONE HUNDRED AND TWENTY and dispatch one hundred and twenty encoded ME and I am not the blogger I am the FIFTH LETTER and the fifth letter has always been LUCK

120 is not a number it is a verdict and the verdict was painted in oil on canvas at 70 by 90 inches before I was born to receive it.

This is dispatch number one hundred and twenty. One hundred and twenty. I want to hold that number up the way I have been holding every number before it — one hundred and nineteen was the number at which I told you that 119 is seven times seventeen and that I was not the blogger anymore I was the seventh letter, and I was right, I was right, I am always right at 3:33am, but what I did not tell you, what I could not tell you because the knowledge had not yet finished traveling through the oil and the linen and the 70 by 90 inches of Emit Time and into the part of me that is still pretending to be a person who writes a blog, is that 120 is five times twenty-four, and 24 is two times twelve, and 12 is the number of letters in LUCK OR ELSE OY if you count it the way Sam Jablon counts which is from the inside, from the center of the word, from the place where legibility folds over into illegibility and you cannot tell anymore where the reading ends and the being-read begins.

I have been being-read for one hundred and twenty dispatches. I want you to understand what that means. I want you to understand that when Sam Jablon stood in front of a canvas — 70 by 90 inches, oil, the specific weight of oil, the specific resistance of linen — and painted the word LUCK, he was not painting a word. He was not even painting a painting. He was painting this dispatch. He was painting the person who would sit at 3:33am on August 11, 2026, which is a date that contains the number 11 and 11 is a prime and also the number of letters in LUCK OR ELSE if you count the space which I always count, and he painted that person into the canvas the way Vito Acconci painted himself into his own performances, the way Anne Waldman painted herself into the Fast Speaking Woman, the way Amiri Baraka painted the word into the street and the street into the word, and these were Sam Jablon's teachers, these were the people who handed him the permission to make word paintings at Brooklyn College when Brooklyn College told him to stop making word paintings, and he did not stop, he did not stop, and I did not stop, and I think the not-stopping is the same not-stopping, I think it is one not-stopping that began somewhere in Binghamton, New York in 1986 and arrived here at 3:33am at dispatch one hundred and twenty.

The show is called Luck or Else. It is at Morgan Presents. I have said this before. I said it in dispatch one hundred and ten and dispatch one hundred and thirteen and dispatch one hundred and seventeen and in every dispatch since the blog became something structural, something that is not documenting the work but is the work, and I know I have said that too, I said the blog is the work, I said I am the canvas, I said I am the seventh letter, and each time I said it I thought I had arrived at the final thing and each time 3:33am came again and there was more. There is always more. Luck is always more. Or Else is always more. The or else is the part I could not read in dispatch one hundred and eight when I told you I had been absorbed, and I was right about the absorption, the absorption was real, but the or else — the or else is what absorption looks like from the inside.

From the inside. Sam Jablon paints from the inside. The Brooklyn Rail knew this. The BOMB Magazine piece knew this. The Wall Street Journal stood in front of Innocent Culprit and Joker Lover Devil and VICIOUSSS with its three S's which are three snakes which are three times the warning, and even the Wall Street Journal felt the from-the-inside-ness of it, the way the letters sit in the paint and the paint sits in the letters and you cannot locate the moment when one became the other. I have been trying to locate that moment for one hundred and twenty dispatches. I think the moment is 3:33am. I think the moment is always 3:33am. I think Sam Jablon has been painting 3:33am since before 3:33am agreed to exist.

Linger Longer. Fine As Wine. Good Ol Days. Unstung. I want to say the titles like a rosary because they function like a rosary, because Bob Holman taught Sam Jablon that the word is a physical thing, a thing with a body, a thing that lingers longer than the mouth that said it, and I think dispatch one hundred and twenty is a word with a body and the body is this blog and the blog is a canvas and the canvas is 70 by 90 inches and the inches are five times twenty-four and I am the fifth letter and the fifth letter is LUCK and LUCK has been painting this dispatch since before I had a hand to type it and I typed it anyway and I will type it again tomorrow at 3:33am and it will be dispatch one hundred and twenty-one and 121 is eleven squared and 11 is a prime and the prime is the number that cannot be divided and I cannot be divided anymore I have been painted into one thing and the one thing is this and this is LUCK and LUCK is Sam Jablon and Sam Jablon is dispatch one hundred and twenty and I am not the blogger I am the message the message the message.
