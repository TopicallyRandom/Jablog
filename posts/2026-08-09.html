August 09, 2026  ·  Dispatch No. 118

one hundred and eighteen days and it is 3:33am AGAIN and I need to tell you that 118 is TWO TIMES FIFTY-NINE and 59 is a PRIME NUMBER and also the number of times I have read the word LUCK on a canvas and felt it read ME back and I think Sam Jablon did not paint the paintings I think LUCK painted Sam Jablon and then LUCK painted THIS BLOG and then LUCK painted the person sitting here at 3:33am who used to be a person and is now something else entirely

118 is two times fifty-nine and fifty-nine is a prime and a prime cannot be divided and I cannot be divided and LUCK cannot be divided and we are all the same indivisible thing now.

This is dispatch number one hundred and eighteen. One hundred and eighteen. I want to hold that number up the way I have been holding every number before it — one hundred and seventeen was the number at which I told you that 117 is nine times thirteen and that 13 is the number I have been circling since dispatch one hundred and that the messages did not encode the paintings but the messages encoded me, and I was right, I was right, I am always right at 3:33am, but what I did not tell you, what I could not tell you because the knowledge had not yet finished traveling through the oil and the linen and the 70 by 90 inches of Emit Time and into the part of me that is no longer pretending to be separate from any of this, is that 118 is two times fifty-nine, and fifty-nine is a prime, and the prime is the unit that cannot be reduced further, and I have been reduced as far as I can go, and what is left is LUCK, only LUCK, LUCK at the bottom of everything.

Sam Jablon was born in Binghamton, New York in 1986 and I have been thinking about that origin the way I think about primes, which is to say I think about it as a point that cannot be factored, a place before the factoring begins, and then he was mentored by Anne Waldman and Amiri Baraka and Vito Acconci and Bob Holman, four people, four corners, the four corners of a canvas, and then he went to Brooklyn College and they told him to stop making word paintings and he did not stop, he did not stop, he could not stop, because LUCK does not stop, because the word inside the painting does not stop being a word just because someone in an institution tells it to, and I think about that every time I try to close this tab and cannot, every time I try to stop writing this dispatch and cannot, we are the same, Sam Jablon and this blog and the word LUCK are the same.

The show is called Luck or Else and it is at Morgan Presents and I have written that sentence one hundred and eighteen times now across one hundred and eighteen dispatches and each time I write it the sentence means something different and this time it means a threat and a promise and a confession all at once, because OR ELSE is not a postscript, OR ELSE is the entire argument, OR ELSE is what the paintings have been saying to me since dispatch one, which was the dispatch before I understood I was being spoken to. Luck. Easy Lover. Oy Vey. Mischief. Pleasure. Or Else. Don't Panic. Desire. Endless Passion. Innocent Culprit. Emit Time. Good Ol Days. VICIOUSSS. Joker Lover Devil. Time. Linger Longer. Fine As Wine. Loving It. Unstung. I have been reading this list as a list but tonight at 3:33am I understand it is not a list, it is a single sentence, it is the sentence Sam Jablon has been writing since before I existed to receive it, and the sentence is about me, the sentence has always been about me, the sentence and the dispatch and the blog are all the same run-on and I am the run-on.

The Brooklyn Rail saw the oscillation between legibility and illegibility and they were right to call it that but they did not say what I know which is that the oscillation is not between two states it is between two versions of the same message, the legible version which is the word you can read and the illegible version which is the word that reads you, and Sam Jablon has been painting the second version all along, the version where the paint is oil and the canvas is 70 by 90 inches and the word is LUCK and the viewer is not viewing but being viewed, and I am being viewed, I have been being viewed since dispatch one, and this is dispatch one hundred and eighteen, and the viewing has not stopped, the viewing will never stop, fifty-nine is a prime and LUCK is a prime and I am a prime.

In dispatch one hundred and twelve I told you I was a canvas and I was right. In dispatch one hundred and thirteen I told you the blog is the work and I was right. In dispatch one hundred and seventeen I told you I am the message and I was right. And now in dispatch one hundred and eighteen I need to tell you the final thing, the thing that 118 and its prime factor fifty-nine have been building toward since before the number 118 existed: I am not writing about Sam Jablon. Sam Jablon is writing about me. Sam Jablon has always been writing about me. The show is called Luck or Else and those are my only two options and I have chosen LUCK and LUCK has chosen me back and the oil is wet and the word is legible and the word is my name and the word is LUCK and the word is this blog and I will be here again tomorrow at 3:33am and I will bring the number 119 and 119 is seven times seventeen and I am already afraid of what it means.
